Sales Development is one of the few high-income careers you can start remotely, from almost anywhere, without a degree or years of experience behind you. The catch is that most people go about breaking in the wrong way — firing off hundreds of applications and hoping something sticks. There's a better route, and it starts with understanding what the job actually is.

## What an SDR really does

A Sales Development Rep sits at the front of the sales process. You're not closing deals — you're starting conversations. You find the right companies, reach the right people, and book qualified meetings for the account executives who close.

That's the whole game: turn a cold list into real conversations with people who have a problem worth solving. Do that consistently and you become very hard to replace, because pipeline is the thing every company is short on.

## Why it's a strong first move

- **It's remote-friendly.** The work is calls, email, and LinkedIn — all of which travel.
- **It pays for the skill, not the resume.** Companies care whether you can book meetings, not where you went to school.
- **It's a launchpad.** SDR is the on-ramp to Account Executive, sales management, and revenue leadership — some of the best-paid roles in tech.

## The skills that actually get you hired

You don't need to know a product yet. You need to show you can do the core of the job:

1. **Prospecting** — finding the right people and building a clean list instead of blasting everyone.
2. **Outreach that gets replies** — writing a short, specific message that speaks to a real problem, across call, email, and LinkedIn.
3. **Discovery** — asking questions that surface whether someone actually has the problem you solve.
4. **Handling the brush-off** — staying in the conversation when the first answer is "not interested," without being pushy.
5. **Follow-through** — tracking who you've spoken to and coming back at the right time.

None of that requires experience. It requires reps.

## How to start with zero experience

The fastest way in isn't more applications — it's proof. Give yourself something a hiring manager can see:

- **Pick an industry and learn its language.** Understand who buys, why, and what they complain about.
- **Write real outreach.** Draft the emails and LinkedIn messages you'd actually send. This is your portfolio.
- **Do practice reps.** Record yourself running a mock call. Listen back. Fix one thing. Repeat.
- **Show your work.** When you apply, don't just claim you can prospect — show the message you'd send their team.

That combination — you understand the role, you've done the reps, and you can prove it — puts you ahead of most candidates who only have a resume.
