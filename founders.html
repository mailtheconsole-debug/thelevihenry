For Founders

You built the platform. Now build the go-to-market.

You've built something that solves a real business problem in a genuinely new way. But building it is only the first step. The harder question is how it reaches its first real users.

What it covers

From “built” to “bought.”

Innovator or disruptor, I've seen some seriously solid platforms. The gap is almost never the product. It's go-to-market. How do you pitch businesses or consumers to actually use it? How do you land your first paying users? That's what we build together, in the order that gets you there.

  • ICP & positioningDefine exactly who you're selling to and why they buy, so every message and demo lands with the right person.
  • Go-to-market strategyChannel selection, pricing model, and the sequence for landing your first paying customers.
  • Your first sales motionThe process, cadence, and scripts a non-sales founder (or your first hire) can actually run.

How it works

Eight modules, from diagnosis to your first customers.

A structured engagement, not a chat. One working session or homework block per module, sequenced so each one builds on the last.

01

Diagnose your startup and you

We read the company and the founder first: what shape the startup is, and where your blind spot sits. Everything after is calibrated from here.

Diagnosis
02

Sharpen ICP and positioning

Build a precise ideal customer profile, then turn it into positioning you can say to a prospect and have them instantly get it.

Positioning
03

Choose the motion

Product-led, sales-led, or the hybrid most startups actually land in, and we name the failure mode you're most at risk of before it bites.

Motion
04

Price and package

Define what you charge for, the value metric, before the price itself. Get that right and the rest of pricing is forgiving.

Pricing
05

Find the channel

Brainstorm every channel, test the two or three most promising cheaply, then go all-in on the one that works instead of spreading thin.

Channels
06

Land the first customers

Make "do things that don't scale" concrete for your product: the hands-on onboarding and discovery call you run yourself for the first 10 to 20 deals.

First customers
07

Scale past founder-led sales

Set the real trigger for your first sales hire, two reps not one, and rehearse the handoff before you actually need it.

Scaling
08

Overlay your vertical

Pull your industry's own rules: how gated it is by compliance, whether it's a committee sale or bottoms-up, and the distribution lever unique to your vertical.

Vertical

The method

Built on proven frameworks, not opinions.

This isn't off-the-cuff advice. The work runs on named, battle-tested frameworks, the same ones behind the startups you already know, applied to your specific product and stage.

  • Ideal customer profileRaceCapital's nine parameters and First Round's signal sources.
  • PositioningApril Dunford's ten-step process from Obviously Awesome.
  • Channel selectionGabriel Weinberg's Bullseye and the 19 traction channels.
  • Market adoptionGeoffrey Moore's Crossing the Chasm: beachhead and whole-product.
  • PricingValue-metric-first pricing, per Patrick Campbell and Lenny's Newsletter.
  • First customersPaul Graham's "do things that don't scale," modeled on Stripe, Airbnb, and Superhuman.

Who it's for

Early-stage startup founders, technical or product-first, who've built a platform but have no go-to-market motion yet: no ideal customer, no pricing or positioning, no first sales process. If you can build it but haven't sold it, this is for you.

The difference

Not the same as sales consulting.

My Sales & Revenue work is for businesses that already have something selling and need a real process behind it. GTM consulting starts a step earlier: it's for founders taking a platform to market for the first time.
